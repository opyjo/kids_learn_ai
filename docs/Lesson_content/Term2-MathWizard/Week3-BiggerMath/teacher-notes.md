# Term 2, Lesson 3: Bigger Math! 🚀

## Teacher's Guide

**Duration:** 60 minutes | **Week:** 3 of 9

## Purpose

Students learn `//`, `%`, `**`, and parentheses through tangible problems. The Fair Share Planner is the primary build. Powers and order of operations are short investigations, not long lists of expressions.

## Objectives

Students can distinguish exact division, complete groups, and leftovers; model repeated multiplication with powers; trace calculation order; and verify programs against physical or hand-calculated results.

## Lesson Flow

| Time | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 0–8 min | Share 23 counters among 5 teams |
| 8–18 min | Connect the model to `//` and `%` |
| 18–33 min | Build and label Fair Share Planner |
| 33–41 min | Three-test investigation |
| 41–48 min | Doubling table and `2 ** n` experiment |
| 48–54 min | Parentheses puzzle |
| 54–58 min | Partner scenario and improvement |
| 58–60 min | Exit ticket |

## Teaching Notes

- Begin with objects or drawings so operators answer a problem students already understand.
- Reinforce the check: `teams * share + leftovers == items` verbally; comparisons are not taught yet, so do not code `==`.
- Explain that `//` keeps complete groups; avoid saying it “rounds down” without context.
- Keep exponent values small enough to reason about before running.
- Have students trace parentheses in stages rather than memorize PEMDAS alone.
- Do not introduce even/odd conditional programs; decisions begin in Term 3.

## Differentiation

**Support:** Use counters and provide the two formulas with blanks.

**Extension:** Design a two-stage packing problem or a power-growth story controlled by one variable.

## Assessment

Look for correct share/leftover explanations, three passed tests including `7 items / 10 teams`, a completed doubling prediction, and an explanation of the parentheses pair.

## Homework Boundary

Homework is one themed model combining the new operators. It should not use interactive input or duplicate the future calculator.

## Looking Ahead

Lesson 4 connects known `input()` to `int()` so another person can operate the math model.
